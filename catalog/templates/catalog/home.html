Item 2: CropSense IoT – Business Model Canvas Essay CropSense IoT is an AgriTech start-up that offers organic farmers innovative soil sensing solutions and satellite-readings analytics to get insight into the properties of their farm and optimize crop production and irrigation. Front Stage Architecture The main Customer Segment for CropSense would be commercial organic farming entities, medium and large-scale, for whom the costs of irrigation and consequent losses of crop volume due to improper care are disastrous. These farmers would be highly interested in a tool that allows them to observe and predict the developments in their plots without the need for large-scale manual interference. The greatest Value Proposition of the start-up would be the provision of analytical information about the crop condition and soil quality based on IoT and satellite data, which will allow optimize the irrigation schedule and, consequently, maximize yield. Thus, the offered solution would serve as a solid basis for farmer decision-making in crop management. As for Channels, they could include not only direct sales via the company’s website and agricultural exhibitions but also cooperation with organic farming communities and agricultural cooperatives. Due to the very nature of the product, the necessity for direct treatment of the infrastructure in the form of sensors’ installation is inevitable; thus, the company should employ field-sell specialists. In terms of Customer Relationship, the provision of complex technical support and installation will be complemented by the provision of remote online dashboards and platforms to track and evaluate the state of the crop. The relationship with the customer will be built primarily at the stage of installation and technical support, and thereafter, the farmer will be able to monitor and manage the system independently. Overall, the suggested approach will help fulfill the customer’s expectations of personalized experience, as the farmer will be directly involved in the management of the system. Back Stage Infrastructure and Financial Ripple Effect Concerning the Key Activities, according to the Value Proposition, they will include sensor installations, processing and analyzing the data retrieved from the sensors and satellites, implementing yield forecasts, optimizing crop irrigation, and providing technical and managerial support to the farmers. As for Key Resources, in addition to the aforementioned support network, it will include IoT sensors, software-developing expertise, data science expertise, servers and software for storing and processing satellite data, and the online platform for analytical dashboard visualization. The key partnerships will be established with hardware developers, satellite data providers and processors, cloud platform suppliers, irrigation equipment manufacturers, and agro-cooperatives and farming associations. Accordingly, the suggested infrastructure will entangle the Cost Structure that will consist of hardware maintenance and development costs, costs of cloud data storage, technical support staff, crop analytics and forecasts, online platform maintenance, and customer support. Thus, the indicators to be validated include the accuracy of the sensor readings and the adequacy of the recommendations based on them. Item 3: FlexSave – User Persona and UX/UI Design Essay For FlexSave, the main problem with depending on demographic data is that it offers only a shallow grasp of users. Details like a user's age and job (for example, a 32-year-old freelance driver) reveal nothing about their motivations, behaviors, and pain points, all of which are essential when defining the product design. For example, a primary persona for FlexSave might be a 32-year-old freelance driver by the name of Niran. Because of his line of work, his income can fluctuate significantly from one month to the next, so at times he has spare money he can put aside, while in other months he may be unable to save at all. His main motivation is to put some money aside for emergencies, since he cannot reliably predict his income or future expenses. Yet he does not wish to commit to a fixed monthly saving schedule, given his unpredictable income. His key pain points might involve limited financial literacy and a fear of losing access to the saved money when emergency costs come up. When it comes to the product's gains, the option to set flexible monthly savings targets and the guarantee that a certain amount stays accessible in an emergency are probably the most important factors for him, along with the chance to invest with minimal financial risk. On the downside, he is very likely to be puzzled by technical financial jargon, and the prospect of not being able to access his savings during emergencies could add further stress, something the product should aim to reduce. Regarding FlexSave's design, these considerations could be incorporated by letting users set dynamic monthly goals rather than following the conventional rigid method. It is particularly important to keep the product intuitive and to limit the use of financial jargon. For instance, the homepage could show the current savings amount, a progress bar toward the emergency fund goal, and a suggested amount to save for that month. In addition, the interface could be built to reduce cognitive load, for example, by clearly showing which amounts are available for emergencies and which amounts are invested. The Golden Rule of Focus matters greatly in product design, since focusing on the most impactful segments can lead to a product that users genuinely want. For FlexSave, applying the Rule means that roughly 80% of design effort and time should go toward the Primary Persona and their needs, while the remaining 20% should be assigned to the Secondary Personas. Therefore, most of the attention should go to building the flexible monthly savings option, the emergency fund, the investment feature, and easy withdrawal. Features aimed at other users' needs (such as the wish to reach financial freedom or a distrust of banks) could be introduced as optional elements. By spending too much time and effort on the needs of the latter, the company would end up with a product that addresses only minor problems for most users.
